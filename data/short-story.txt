Elena, a tired night attendant in her forties, runs the lost-and-found office at the city tram depot. She has kept one rule for twenty years: nothing leaves the counter without proof that it is yours. Tonight is the office's last night. At dawn, everything unclaimed goes to the incinerator.

Rain hammers the depot roof. Elena tapes shut the last cardboard boxes and stacks them by the door, each one stamped UNCLAIMED.

Just before midnight the bell rings. Sami, a ten-year-old boy in a yellow raincoat, stands dripping at the counter. He has come alone. He asks for a red wool scarf his mother lost on the number 9 tram a year ago. His mother died in the spring. He has no ticket stub, no receipt, nothing that proves the scarf was hers.

Elena knows the scarf. She has seen it in the unclaimed box a hundred times. But she tells Sami what she tells everyone: no proof, no claim. Sami does not argue. He nods, pulls up his hood, and walks back out into the rain.

Alone, Elena opens the unclaimed box. The red scarf is folded on top. Pinned inside the fold is a paper luggage tag in faded blue ink: For Sami, when he is cold. She reads it twice. The proof was in the box all along, and she sent him away.

Elena grabs her coat and runs into the tram yard. The night is black and the rails shine with rain. The last tram is pulling out. She bangs on the doors until the driver, an old man named Osei, lets her on. She sits soaked in the empty carriage, the scarf in her lap, counting the stops.

At the terminus, under a flickering shelter light, Sami sits alone on the bench, waiting for a bus that will not come until morning. Elena kneels in front of him. She does not ask for proof. She wraps the red scarf around his neck and shows him the tag. Sami reads his mother's handwriting and finally cries. Elena stays beside him on the bench until his aunt's car pulls up, headlights cutting through the rain.

At dawn, the depot is silent. The incinerator truck backs up to the office door and the workers load the unclaimed boxes. Elena hands over her keys and leaves her name badge on the empty counter.

On the first morning tram, Sami sits by the window in his yellow raincoat with the red scarf wrapped twice around his neck, watching the city wake up.
